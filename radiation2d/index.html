THE SCIENCE & THE PEOPLE

A field you can follow.

This educational simulation follows emitted wavelets from a single moving charge. Each travels at light speed in a direction set by relativistic aberration. Connecting their history reveals the electric field lines.

Tsumoru Shintake — original Radiation2D

The original visualization and wavelet method are described in “New Real-Time Simulation Technique for Synchrotron and Undulator Radiations,” LINAC2002, pp. 659–661, and “Real-time animation of synchrotron radiation,” NIM A 507 (2003), 89–92. The original screenshots inspired this display. Original software & manuals ↗

Reading the picture

Cyan lines show electric field geometry; their crowding is qualitative. Pink circles show information propagating from earlier particle positions—even a stationary charge has these wavefronts, without radiating energy. Yellow marks the charge's path.

The oscillator is a single charge moving back and forth: a dipole-radiation demonstration. The separate Dipole preset represents a 30° bending magnet. It repeats only after the charge has moved outside the visible area. Repeat pass restarts that demonstration with fresh incoming field history; it is a replay, rather than a physical return path. In the Wiggler preset, K sets peak transverse momentum in units of mc; K is limited below γβ to preserve forward motion at the selected speed. “Magnet periods” sets the number of complete periods in the magnetic region. The view stays fixed in the laboratory frame. In the Wiggler preset, the magnetic region occupies the middle half of the screen, with straight entry and exit sections. Repeat pass restarts the demonstration with fresh incoming field history when the charge reaches the right edge.

This is a finite-history visualization of a planar slice of a 3D field. It does not calculate calibrated radiation power, spectra, or multi-particle interference. Brightness is for legibility, not a power measurement.
