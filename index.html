SCIENCE & SOURCES

Models and sources

This is a teaching companion to Synrad tutorial by Márton Ady and Gabriel Saretti (CERN), CAS Prague, 16 November 2026. Slide numbers refer to the 44-page lecture supplied with this project.

The spectrum and energy-resolved vertical polarization use numerical integrals of modified Bessel functions. Local angular polarization maps integrate over photon energy and share one intensity scale; their 7/8 and 1/8 fractions apply only after integration over all directions. Dipole readouts use uniform-field magnetic rigidity. Quadrupole power holds an entrance-plane round Gaussian beam fixed along the magnet; its RMS-field critical energy is indicative, not an exact spectrum. Reference-frame surfaces and plot slices use the classical instantaneous angular-power distribution for transverse acceleration, with each frame normalized to its own peak. Surface radius is not photon position. The horizontal fan is a uniform bend convolved with a Gaussian approximation to the emission cone.

Material results are illustrative: free-electron grazing Fresnel reflection and an E⁻³ attenuation law omit absorption edges, real optical tables, fluorescence and scattering. The rough-surface fan is qualitative. Insertion-device spectra illustrate finite-train interference and a smooth transition to a broad wiggler envelope; they are not a beamline calculation.

The Cavity radiation extension transports equal-energy packets from ideal longitudinal acceleration through an axisymmetric absorbing cavity. It uses straight rays and first-wall interception, with one spatial scale. It does not solve cavity boundary fields, RF phase, reflection, diffraction or interference. Port fractions are geometrical estimates over emission time and full solid angle.

All geometry and animation speeds are schematic. Values in the controls and plots carry the physical units. These models are intended for learning.
